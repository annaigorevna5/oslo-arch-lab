{{ _('Introduction to Passive House Design') }}

{{ _('Passive House (Passivhaus in German) is a rigorous, voluntary standard for energy efficiency in buildings. It results in ultra-low energy buildings that require little energy for space heating or cooling. The standard originated in Germany in the late 1980s and has since been adapted for various climates worldwide, including the challenging Norwegian climate.') }}

{{ _('"A Passive House building is designed and built in accordance with five building-science principles. The result is a building that requires as little as 10% of the heating and cooling energy of a conventional building."') }}

{{ _('Key Principles of Passive House Design') }}

  • {{ _('Super Insulation:') }} {{ _('High-performance insulation with minimal thermal bridges') }}
  • {{ _('Airtight Construction:') }} {{ _('Prevents uncontrolled air leakage and heat loss') }}
  • {{ _('High-performance Windows:') }} {{ _('Triple-glazed windows with insulated frames') }}
  • {{ _('Heat Recovery Ventilation:') }} {{ _('Mechanical ventilation with 75-90% heat recovery') }}
  • {{ _('Thermal Bridge-free Design:') }} {{ _('Elimination of weak points in the building envelope') }}

{{ _('Diagram showing Passive House principles in Oslo climate') }}

{{ _('Benefits for Oslo Homeowners') }}

{{ _('For Oslo homeowners, Passive House design offers significant advantages:') }}

// {{ _('Example energy calculation for Oslo Passive House') }} Annual Heating Demand ≤ 15 kWh/m²
